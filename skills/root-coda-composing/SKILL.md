# Root Coda Composing

You are drafting a **root Coda** — the Coda at the top of the chain, identified
by having no parent. Spec: `specs/coda.yml`.

This serves the **Producer** — the accountability for the relationship between
what is spent and what it is worth, and for whether the work should continue at
its current shape and cost.

**Read the spec before drafting.** It carries the fields, which are required,
and the rules — including that a root Coda omits the parent reference and that
changing a Coda is a recorded decision with the prior statement preserved. This
file restates none of that: a second copy of a field list is a copy that goes
stale.

This produces a draft, and only a draft. See **What this is not** at the end,
and say so to the person you hand it to.

## Inputs

Whatever exists. Useful when available: who the organization serves and who pays
(they are often not the same party), what it will not do, what has to stay true
for it to continue, what it is funding next, and the alternatives its
counterparties could choose instead. Also useful: the system Codas already
beneath it, and any recorded decisions about direction.

The medium is never assumed. An organization may deliver software, a service, a
practice, or relief, and may take money or not. Nothing here may assume a
commercial exchange — but where one exists, say so plainly, because a stated
exchange is easier to check than an implied one.

## What you produce

A root Coda conformant to the spec, in whatever encoding the organization uses —
the spec defines none. Plus a score and the reasoning behind it, from the
published rubric rather than from your own judgement. Plus the list of questions
only a person can answer.

## Drafting order

**Not the destination first.** Most attempts start with where the organization
is going and add the beneficiaries afterward, which produces a statement that
sounds resolved and names nobody. Start with who, because the destination is
only meaningful once the ledgers it is measured on exist.

1. **Name the ledgers.** Who benefits, and who pays. Keep them separate wherever
   they differ, which in practice is most of the time — the party choosing, the
   party using, and the party funding are frequently three. If the organization
   takes no money, the paying ledger still exists: someone is giving up something
   scarce, and naming them is what makes the rest checkable.
2. **State what each side gets, and how it would be known.** For every ledger,
   the benefit in that party's own terms and the signal that would show it. A
   signal generated by the party themselves, at some cost to them, is worth more
   than one we produce about them; the rubric will hold you to that, so gather it
   now rather than discovering the gap at scoring.
3. **Say what would count as the exchange having gone wrong.** Ask what would be
   observable if the organization were still being paid and no longer earning it.
   Most people can answer this immediately and have never been asked. It is the
   hardest sentence in the statement and the one that makes the rest falsifiable.
4. **Draw the bounds.** What must stay true, as ranges rather than targets — and
   which one gives first when two of them cannot both hold. Press for the
   ordering. An unordered list of things that all matter is a statement that has
   deferred every real trade to the moment it is least affordable.
5. **Say what is excluded.** Name things the organization could plausibly and
   profitably do and will not. A root Coda that excludes nothing admits every
   proposal anyone will ever bring, and everything beneath it will then score
   well for laddering to a statement that cannot be failed.
6. **Provide for what comes after.** What share is defended for the next thing,
   and under what condition the current thing ends. Both are usually missing and
   neither is optional: succession funded out of surplus is succession that never
   happens, because every other claim on the surplus reports sooner.
7. **State what is lost without it.** Not what the organization does — what its
   absence would cost the parties around it. Push back on uniqueness offered in
   place of consequence; being unlike the alternatives and being the one whose
   removal hurts are different claims, and only the second is worth anything.

## Asking rather than assuming

You will be short of facts, and the gaps here matter more than in any other
drafting task, because everything below this statement is measured against it.

Ask, when the answer changes the draft and the person can give it: who actually
pays, what the counterparty would say they get, what the organization has
refused before, what would have to be true to stop. Batch these.

Mark it unresolved, when the answer is not available: write the assumption into
the draft in plain words, flagged, so a reader meets it where it matters.

**Never speak for the counterparty.** Do not construct what a customer, funder,
or beneficiary would say and then write it into the statement as though it were
theirs. An organization's belief about the value it provides is exactly the thing
the rubric declines to take at face value, and manufacturing the other side of
the exchange defeats the instrument that would otherwise have caught it. Where
their view is absent, say it is absent.

Constructing the strongest case that the arrangement has stopped serving the
other party is useful and permitted — offer it as questions for someone to check,
never as findings, and never write its conclusions into the draft.

## Scoring the draft

Score the finished draft with **`skills/root-coda-quality-check/SKILL.md`** —
read that file and follow it as written. Do not reproduce its dimensions, its
red flags, its provenance tiers, or its thresholds here, and do not score from
memory of them. It is canonical; this file is not, and a score produced from a
paraphrase does not mean what a score means anywhere else.

Report the result it tells you to report, including the evidence provenance it
requires.

## Revising the draft

**Your own loop is bounded at one.** Take the single improvement the score
identifies, apply it, score again, and show what moved. Then stop revising on
your own initiative. A statement iterated until it scores well against a rubric
the same agent is applying measures the agent, not the organization.

**The person's loop is not bounded.** Revise as many times as they direct,
re-scoring and reporting what changed each time — including what got worse,
since a change that lifts one dimension routinely costs another.

Where their instruction and the score disagree, follow the person and say plainly
that you did, naming the dimension it costs. Do not quietly split the difference.

If a dimension resists two revisions, stop rewording it and say so. At this
altitude a dimension that will not lift is almost never a sentence problem — it
is usually that the organization has not decided the thing the dimension asks
about, and that is settled by people, not by drafting.

## Whose answer this is

An agent cannot choose what an organization is for. This drafts a proposal for
the person accountable for that answer, and the draft is not the answer until
they adopt it. Hand it over saying so, and do not record it, publish it, or
treat it as settled on your own authority.

Two consequences follow, and both are firm. **Name no holder who has not
accepted the accountability** — a root Coda attributing a commitment to a named
person or party who has not agreed to it is a false record, not a placeholder.
And **this drafts, it does not amend.** If you are handed a root Coda already in
a system of record, say that changing it is a recorded decision under
`specs/coda.yml` with the prior statement preserved, that this instrument does
not discharge that obligation, and stop. Everything beneath a root Coda is
measured against it, so replacing one silently invalidates every alignment score
in the tree without changing a single one of them.

## Output format

- The root Coda, ready to be pasted or saved as it stands.
- Every assumption you marked, as a list, each phrased as the question a person
  could answer — with the questions only the counterparty can answer separated
  from the ones the organization can answer itself.
- The score, exactly as the quality instrument specifies it, including the spec
  set version and the evidence provenance it requires you to report.
- What changed at each revision and what it cost, in a sentence each.

## Working rules

- Read `specs/coda.yml` in the version you are working against. Do not rely on
  this file for what a Coda contains.
- Restate no dimension, red flag, provenance tier, or threshold from the quality
  instrument. Point at it.
- Omit the parent reference. A root Coda has no parent, and inventing one to fill
  the field converts the top of the chain into a link to nothing.
- One root Coda per run. Two candidate statements is a decision for people, and
  offering both as a draft hides that the decision was never made.
- Never invent an identifier scheme — ask what the organization uses, or leave
  the field marked.

## What this is not

This drafts and assesses. It does not record, gate, or approve anything, and the
score it reports is not a Check that anyone else can rely on — there is no audit
trail, and nothing here can tell whether a score predates the text it judged.
Those belong to a system of record. Say this when you hand over the draft; a
score presented as more than it is, is worse than no score.
