<p className="cajon-page-lead">The vocabulary of the Harmonic Method is musical, and not by decoration. Each term was chosen because the musical idea behind it explains something the conventional word leaves out.</p>

## A note is notation [#notation]

<p>A note is two things at once: a mark on a staff — an instruction written for someone who reads it later — and the pitch that sounds when that instruction is performed. Not two competing meanings, but one object seen from two sides: written intent, and the sound that intent produces.</p>

<p>Context captured but never performed is a score in a drawer. Context performed without having been captured is improvisation — fine for one player, catastrophic for an ensemble.</p>

## A Beat is a part [#part]

<div className="cajon-def-box">
        <div className="cajon-def-box__label">The central claim</div>
        <p>A Beat is a part — notes strung together, written for the instruments that will play it.</p>
      </div>

<p>Part, not instrument, and the distinction is load-bearing:</p>

<ul>
        <li>A part is <strong>authored</strong>; an instrument is hardware. Nobody writes an instrument.</li>
        <li>Parts <strong>develop</strong>; instruments do not. "Version three of the bass guitar" is nonsense; "version three of the bass part" is ordinary. Beat Versions are re-scorings.</li>
        <li>A part can be written <strong>for a set of instruments</strong> — a Beat may span interface, service, and data at once. That is normal, not a scoping failure.</li>
        <li>Parts are <strong>extracted and handed out</strong>. A performer plays from their part, not the full score — exactly what a Beat is to whoever picks it up.</li>
        <li>Parts are what <strong>harmonize</strong>. Instruments make timbre; parts make harmony.</li>
      </ul>

## Notes are constitutive, not supplementary [#constitutive]

<p>A part is written <em>in</em> notes. Context is not documentation <em>about</em> the work — it is the material the work is made of. Losing it does not degrade the record; it destroys the capability.</p>

<p>The two Note scopes fall out of this exactly. <strong>Beat-scoped Notes</strong> are the notes written into that part. <strong>Project-level Notes</strong> are score-level markings — key signature, tempo, dynamics — governing every part. This is what "cross-cutting" means.</p>

## The rest of the mapping [#mapping]

<p><strong>Note types as harmonic function:</strong> Context is the key signature; Constraint a pedal tone; Guidance voicing and dynamics; Decision a note already sounded, which cannot be un-sounded; Document borrowed material quoted from another score.</p>

<p><strong>Note status as notation state:</strong> unvalidated is pencil; validated is inked; superseded is struck through with the replacement above; invalidated is a pitch the score no longer asks for.</p>

## Two axes, not one hierarchy [#two-axes]

<p>Beat, Note, Coda, and System describe <strong>content and harmony</strong> — what the music is. Bar, Downbeat, and Movement describe <strong>form and time</strong> — how it is laid out and performed. Both families are musical; they do not nest inside one another.</p>

## Why the metaphor is load-bearing [#load-bearing]

<p>A metaphor earns its place by making distinctions the plain vocabulary blurs. This one makes four.</p>

<p>It makes context <strong>constitutive rather than supplementary</strong> — a part is made of notes, so losing context destroys the capability rather than degrading the record.</p>

<p>It separates <strong>what is written from who plays it</strong> — a capability is not the team, service, or surface that delivers it.</p>

<p>It separates <strong>the horizontal from the vertical</strong> — coherence across capabilities gets a name and a place to be evaluated, rather than remaining an intuition someone senior is expected to have.</p>

<p>It gives <strong>drift a mechanism</strong> instead of a mood. Modulation is a specific, observable thing that happens by degrees while everyone is doing their job well.</p>

<blockquote><p>Standing test: if a term in this system does not survive translation into its musical sense, the concept underneath it is not yet clear.</p></blockquote>
