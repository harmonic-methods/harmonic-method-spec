<p className="cajon-page-lead">Delivery needs a rhythm, not only a structure. An ensemble that cannot hear each other still needs a shared sense of time, and tempo is the part of coherence that can be agreed in advance rather than negotiated in the moment.</p>

## Bar [#bar]

<p>The recurring planning window a team delivers within. Its start and Cutoff are explicit instants. Crossing the Cutoff is the occasion for the Conductor to tie off unfinished work before a Downbeat is taken.</p>

<p>In music, "bar" and "measure" are the same word, and a measure is never defined by its bar lines — only by what sounds inside them. Two windows of identical length are entirely different music depending on their content, which is the argument against comparing time-boxes as if they were units of anything.</p>

## Downbeat [#downbeat]

<p>The recalibration anchor: an honest, frozen reading of where the work actually stands against its destination, set at a Bar boundary.</p>

<p>A conductor does not restart the piece to fix drifting tempo; they mark the next downbeat and everyone lands together on it. Recalibration is additive and non-destructive, because the passage already played still happened.</p>

## Set your cadence [#config]

<p>Choose a cadence that fits the work. Longer periods can contain shorter Bars, and an implementation can offer settings for additional progress reviews.</p>

<p>Set once and held, so the pulse belongs to the ensemble rather than being renegotiated each cycle.</p>

## Why tempo is set in advance [#why-advance]

<p>Recalibration that only happens when a deadline forces it is not recalibration — it is escalation. Fixing the cadence ahead of time means the honest reading arrives on a schedule the team chose, while there is still room to act on what it says.</p>
