<p className="cajon-page-lead">The Harmonic Method is a method for building software where intent is explicit, durable, and preserved across handoffs.</p>

## The problem it addresses [#problem]

<p>Software work fails at the seams. Strategic intent decays every time work is handed off — from strategy to product, product to engineering, engineering to execution, and increasingly from humans to agents.</p>

<p>This accumulated loss is decision debt: the gap between what an organization meant to build and what it can still account for having built.</p>

## Naming [#naming]

<p>The method is singular: <strong>The Harmonic Method</strong>. Discipline-specific applications may follow — the Harmonic Method for Software Engineering, for hard tech R&amp;D — but the method itself is one thing and is always spoken in the singular. Precedent: the Suzuki Method has variants per instrument and is never pluralized in speech.</p>

<p>This site documents the Harmonic Method, stewarded by Codazen. Composition, orchestration, and conducting are ordinary English here, describing what a Composer, an Orchestrator, and a Conductor do.</p>

## Why "method" and not "framework" [#method-not-framework]

<p>"Framework" is crowded, reads as engineering jargon, and implies scaffolding you build inside. "Method" implies a practice that is learned, taught, and certified — which matches the intended adoption path through practitioner certification rather than tool adoption.</p>

## Structure of the method [#structure]

<p>The method defines six roles that act on intent. The functions are constant. What varies is the altitude at which a given function is scoped.</p>

<p>Altitude is a property of the work, not of the role. Because the primitives are self-similar — a system can be evaluated for distinctiveness and coherence the same way a Beat can — altitude is recursion, not a special case. Every role is therefore defined as "perform this function at the altitude to which I am scoped."</p>

## Adoption note [#adoption]

<p>The six roles are functions, not headcount. In a small organization one person may hold three of them. The diagnostic value of the method is not in creating new job titles — it is in revealing which function is currently unowned. Unowned functions are where decision debt accumulates.</p>
