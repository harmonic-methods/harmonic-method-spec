<p className="cajon-page-lead">Parts have to reach performers. This is the Orchestrator's function in its strictest sense: deciding which forces carry which material, and in what order.</p>

Tools can represent assignments, temporary claims, and sequencing in different ways. The practices below illustrate one way to make coordination visible.

## Forces [#forces]

<p>The performers available, human and AI. Not interchangeable, and the difference is not skill.</p>

<p>A human handed a part in the wrong key will usually stop and ask. An agent will sight-read it fluently and hand back confident, well-made work that does not belong. Assignment has to account for which failure mode it is inviting.</p>

## Sections [#sections]

<p>The Layers a part is scored for — grouped by architectural role rather than technology, the way an orchestra groups by family rather than by manufacturer. A part written for three sections is still one part.</p>

## Assignment [#assignment]

<p>Durable ownership — who is responsible, however long it takes. A question about accountability rather than activity. It survives someone being on holiday.</p>

## Claim [#claim]

<p>Who has the stand <em>right now</em>. Heartbeat-backed and auto-released when stale — the live signal that someone is actively playing this passage, so concurrent actors see it is taken before starting duplicate work.</p>

<p>The Claim is the clearest illustration of what distributed delivery has to reconstruct. <strong>An orchestra has no need for one</strong> — you can see who has their instrument up. Where performers cannot see each other, two actors picking up the same work stays invisible until both have finished and only one result is usable.</p>

<blockquote><p>Assignment and claiming answer different questions, and collapsing them is a common and expensive mistake. "Who owns this?" and "who is playing it right now?" have different answers most of the time, and a system that records only one will be wrong about the other.</p></blockquote>

## Handoff [#handoff]

<p>Passing work between agents, or between an agent and a human, without losing the context that explains why it looks the way it does so far. The seam where context degrades if nothing is holding it.</p>

## Dependency sequencing [#sequencing]

<p>Explicit ordering across agents working related Work Items, so they build on each other's output instead of colliding or overwriting shared assumptions.</p>

## Why this is explicit [#why-explicit]

<p>A co-located ensemble gets most of this for free: who is playing, who just changed something, what everyone else is doing. A distributed ensemble of humans and agents gets none of it. Every coordination element here exists because a signal that is ambient in a rehearsal room has to be deliberately constructed in a distributed one.</p>
