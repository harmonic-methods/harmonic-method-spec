<p className="cajon-page-lead">The Conductor's actual job, and the function most often left unowned. Every element here answers a question that only exists during execution, and that no performer is positioned to answer from inside their own part.</p>

## An ensemble that cannot hear itself [#problem]

<p>A conductor in front of an orchestra is not primarily beating time — a competent ensemble keeps time on its own. The conductor is <em>listening</em>: for balance, for a section rushing, for an entrance about to be missed.</p>

<p>That job is possible because everyone in the room hears everyone else. Musicians self-correct continuously and unconsciously against what they hear, and almost all ensemble coherence is produced this way rather than by instruction.</p>

<p>A delivery ensemble has none of it. Performers work from their own extracted part and whatever context travelled with it. AI performers have the sharpest version of the problem: an agent sight-reads anything put in front of it and plays it fluently and confidently whether or not it is in the right key. It does not hear the other parts, so it cannot notice that its output clashes — clashing is a property of the whole, and an agent only ever holds its part.</p>

<blockquote><p>The Conductor function is the discipline of making explicit everything a co-located ensemble gets for free from being in the same room.</p></blockquote>

## The signals [#signals]

<ul>
        <li><strong>Pulse</strong>: is the ensemble together? Not whether work is being done, but whether it is being done in time with the rest. An ensemble can be fully occupied and thoroughly apart.</li>
        <li><strong>Drift</strong>: has the key moved? Coda drift is <em>modulation without a new destination</em> — the context in effect shifted while the stated destination did not. Nothing was abandoned and nothing decided. Measured rather than sensed, because it is imperceptible from inside a single part.</li>
        <li><strong>Harmony</strong>: the vertical read, taken at a Downbeat. A slice across every part at one instant is a chord, consonant or not, and no individual part reveals which.</li>
        <li><strong>Cueing</strong>: triage and next actions. A cue is not a status report — it is directed at a specific performer, about a specific imminent thing, in time to act on it.</li>
        <li><strong>Wrong notes</strong>: diagnostic warnings, made audible rather than logged and forgotten. The value is not the record; it is that someone hears it while the piece is still playing.</li>
      </ul>

## Drift forces a choice [#drift]

<p>Naming drift as modulation makes the remedy obvious and the choice unavoidable. Either re-establish the original key, or write a new Coda in the key the work has actually moved to.</p>

<p>What is not available is continuing to play in two keys and calling the dissonance a scheduling problem.</p>

## This does not contradict the score [#score]

<p>The Composer's artifacts are what let independent performers stay coherent without anyone standing over each one. The score removes the need to dictate <strong>what to play</strong> — that is written down, and dictating it again is waste.</p>

<p>What a score cannot tell you is <strong>how the playing is going</strong>. Balance, entrances, tempo, drift are properties of the performance, not of the notation, and they are the entirety of what the Conductor adds.</p>

<blockquote><p>A conductor who has to tell the ensemble what the notes are is compensating for a bad score. A conductor who listens and cues is doing the job the score cannot do.</p></blockquote>

## Not a process [#not-a-process]

<p>These signals are in play continuously and simultaneously, not as stages that complete. The failure mode of most delivery process is treating them as phases — assigning at the start of a cycle, listening at the end of one, and being surprised by what the gap produced.</p>

<p>What the method asks is narrower and harder: that each signal exists explicitly, is current, and is available to whoever must act on it.</p>

<blockquote><p>Listening degrades most quietly. Assignment failures announce themselves and tempo failures show on a calendar, but an ensemble that has stopped listening sounds busy right up until the recording is played back.</p></blockquote>
