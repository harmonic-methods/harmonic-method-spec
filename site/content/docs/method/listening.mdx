Listening means staying close enough to the work to notice when reality differs from the plan. The Conductor gathers evidence from people, agents, and delivered changes, then helps the team act within agreed scope, capacity, and authority.

The output is a next action, an owner, or a decision request—not just a status report.

## Start with a shared picture [#problem]

Keep the current Coda, active Beat Version plans, constraints, and owners accessible.

  <CalloutDescription>Review a demo, a change under review, a test result, or a conversation with the person blocked. A progress label alone rarely explains what needs attention.</CalloutDescription>

## What to watch [#signals]

| Signal | What to check | Conductor's response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work is waiting | A dependency, review, or handoff has no clear next owner. | Confirm who acts next, what they need, and when to check back. |
| The result misses the intent | A demo or change passes its checks but does not satisfy the intended outcome. | Clarify the gap against the Coda and acceptance criteria; agree on a correction. |
| The plan no longer fits capacity | New requests or unexpected effort threaten current commitments. | Surface the tradeoff to Orchestrator instead of silently expanding the plan. |
| A decision is no longer being followed | An implementation contradicts an active constraint or recorded choice. | Establish whether the decision was missed or new evidence challenges it; correct or escalate. |
| A warning keeps recurring | The same blocker, failed check, or unanswered decision appears again. | Name the unresolved cause and decision owner; follow through until it is addressed. |

## Run a short execution check [#score]

Use this during normal coordination, at a handoff, or when new evidence arrives. A short daily check may suit active delivery; adapt the frequency to the work.

1. **Compare progress with the intended outcome.** What can the team demonstrate, and what remains uncertain?
2. **Find the next impediment.** What is blocked, waiting for review, or likely to exceed the agreed plan?
3. **Agree on an action and owner.** Make the handoff explicit and choose when to follow up.
4. **Preserve what changed.** Capture a decision or new constraint in a Note where it informs the work. Update the execution record so the next person or agent starts with current context.

Resolve problems within your authority as they arise. Use the [agreed cadence](/docs/method/cadence) to reconcile the broader picture; urgent decisions should not wait for that review.

## Respond to drift [#drift]

Drift is a gap between the stated outcome or decisions and what is actually happening. First identify the evidence: which result, assumption, or constraint differs, and what it affects.

If the agreed intent still holds, help the team correct execution. If new evidence challenges the intent, take a decision request to the role that owns it:

- **Composer:** capability definition, system boundaries, or the Coda expressing them.
- **Orchestrator:** allocation, dependencies, capacity, or commitments beyond the agreed envelope.
- **Producer:** investment, cost, or whether continuing is worthwhile.
- **Director:** strategic direction or the assumptions behind it.

Bring the finding, its impact, feasible options, and your recommendation. Record the decision and rationale. A Coda change preserves the prior statement; it should not be rewritten merely to make current work appear aligned.

## Example: a new request exceeds capacity [#not-a-process]

A team is delivering a client approval capability. A new request adds another approval stage, and the engineer says it will displace work already planned.

The Conductor checks whether the request fits the agreed capability and asks Composer to resolve any ambiguity. The Conductor then brings Orchestrator the capacity impact and options: defer the request, replace planned work, or seek additional capacity. Producer joins if the choice needs more investment.

Once the responsible roles decide, the Conductor records the rationale, confirms the revised plan and owners, and checks the next demo against that decision. The request becomes a visible tradeoff instead of an unspoken commitment.

## Use agents to prepare the review [#assistants]

An agent can summarize changes, compare work with the current plan, flag contradictions, and draft follow-up questions. Ask it to cite the evidence and separate observations from assumptions. The Conductor checks the findings and owns the response; the agent acts only within explicitly delegated limits.

The published [`coda-alignment-check`](https://github.com/harmonic-methods/harmonic-method-spec/blob/main/skills/coda-alignment-check/SKILL.md) helps review a Coda chain, and [`downbeat-taking`](https://github.com/harmonic-methods/harmonic-method-spec/blob/main/skills/downbeat-taking/SKILL.md) captures an anchor for later comparison.
