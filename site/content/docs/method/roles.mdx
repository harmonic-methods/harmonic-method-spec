title: "The five functions"
description: "Composer, Orchestrator, Conductor, Producer, and Promoter — the five functions of The Harmonic Method, each scoped to an altitude."
---

<p className="cajon-page-lead">The Harmonic Method defines five functions that act on intent. Each is scoped to an altitude. The function does not change with altitude; only its unit of work does.</p>

## Composer — establishes intent [#composer]

<p>Defines what must become true and why, and the constraints that bound it. Owns the North Star and the definition of the work. The Composer's output is the durable artifact everything downstream depends on.</p>

## Orchestrator — turns intent into structure [#orchestrator]

<p>Sequencing, dependencies, and assignment of performers. This is where the method departs from conventional practice: agents are first-class performers, not tools. The Orchestrator composes the arrangement across humans and agents alike.</p>

## Conductor — holds coherence during execution [#conductor]

<p>Real-time judgment when reality diverges from the plan. The Conductor detects drift and reanchors the work to intent. This function is continuous, not episodic.</p>

## Producer — owns value [#producer]

<p>Decides whether the work earns its keep: what gets funded, and what gets made. The Producer is the business function of the method and one of two that do not make the music directly.</p>

<p>The Producer uses evidence of value and cost to decide whether work still earns its place. An implementation can provide measures such as Value Velocity.</p>

## Promoter — carries the work to its audience [#promoter]

<p>Owns whether the work is known, understood, and taken up. The Producer decides what gets made; the Promoter makes sure the people it was made for actually arrive.</p>

<p>In music the promoter books the room and fills it. A performance nobody hears is not a performance, and a capability nobody adopts is indistinguishable from one that was never built. This is the function that closes that gap — positioning, announcement, documentation, enablement, and the read on whether any of it landed.</p>

<hr className="hm-hr" />

## Altitude [#altitude]

<p>Altitude is declared in the substance layer, not baked into role definitions. The same five functions operate at each altitude with a different unit of work.</p>

<div className="hm-docs-table" tabIndex={0} role="region" aria-label="Comparison table">
<table className="hm-table">
        <thead><tr><th>Altitude</th><th>Unit of work</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Beat or track</td><td>A discrete piece of work</td></tr>
          <tr><td>System</td><td>A system — evaluated for distinctiveness and coherence exactly as a Beat would be</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Portfolio</td><td>The set of systems</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
</div>

<p>The Producer therefore exists at multiple altitudes: a system producer asks whether a given system earns its keep; a portfolio producer asks it across the set.</p>

<div className="cajon-callout">Test for correct abstraction: each role's definition must be writable without naming a specific altitude. If a definition requires the word "portfolio" to make sense, the abstraction has not taken.</div>

## Mapping to conventional roles [#mapping]

<p>Offered as an adoption aid, not as an equivalence.</p>

<div className="hm-docs-table" tabIndex={0} role="region" aria-label="Comparison table">
<table className="hm-table">
        <thead><tr><th>Function</th><th>Closest conventional role</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Composer</td><td>Product management — the PM or product owner defining what should be true and why</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Orchestrator</td><td>Engineering leadership or tech lead</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Conductor</td><td>Delivery leadership — the engineering manager, or whoever holds the line when reality diverges</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Producer</td><td>Product GM or business owner at system altitude; CTO or CEO at portfolio altitude</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Promoter</td><td>Product marketing, developer relations, or enablement — whoever owns adoption rather than delivery</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
</div>

<p>These are functions, not headcount. The diagnostic question is not "who has this title" but "is this function owned." Unowned functions are where decision debt accumulates.</p>

<hr className="hm-hr" />

## Supporting the functions with agents [#assistants]

<p>An agent can hold the context and prepare material for a person responsible for a function. The Composer's assistant drafts the Coda and the Beats and runs the quality check. The Orchestrator's proposes the arrangement and the sequencing. The Conductor's watches the signals and raises the cue. The Producer's assembles the value read. The Promoter's prepares what goes out.</p>

<p>An assistant does not hold the function. It cannot decide that the work earns its keep, or that the key has moved — it prepares the material those decisions are made from, which is the part that scales.</p>
