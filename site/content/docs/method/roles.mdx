---
title: Harmonic Method Roles
description: Six roles connect strategic direction, system definition, execution, investment, and adoption.
---

Each role carries a distinct accountability. One person may hold several roles; each needs an identifiable person who answers for its decisions. The specification calls these accountabilities **functions**.

## Director — owns strategic direction [#director]

Sets the direction, the outcomes that matter, and the assumptions behind them. Watches changes in customer needs, business conditions, and evidence to decide whether that direction still holds.

The Director sets or revises the strategic Coda in their scope. Composer defines the systems and capabilities that serve it; Producer decides which investments justify their cost. A coherent system or a positive local return can still serve an outdated strategy. Director owns recognizing and correcting that problem.

## Composer — defines the system and its intent [#composer]

Defines what a system or capability must make true, why it matters, and the constraints and boundaries that keep it coherent. Expresses that intent through Codas, Capability Beats, and the Notes that explain them.

Composer turns strategic direction into a coherent definition of the work. If the definition exposes a conflict with strategy, Composer brings it to Director rather than silently changing direction.

## Orchestrator — arranges the work and resources [#orchestrator]

Plans sequencing, dependencies, capacity, and the division of work across people and agents. Defines workflows, assignment policies, and what can run unattended or needs human review.

Orchestrator sets the execution arrangement. When work needs resources or commitments beyond the agreed envelope, Orchestrator decides the allocation response with Producer where investment approval is needed.

## Conductor — leads day-to-day execution [#conductor]

Keeps people and agents moving toward the agreed outcome. Applies assignment policies, coordinates handoffs, resolves blockers, and keeps decisions and constraints in force while work happens.

Conductor adjusts execution within agreed scope, capacity, and authority. Changes beyond those boundaries go to the appropriate role: resource allocation to Orchestrator, system definition to Composer, investment to Producer, and strategic direction to Director. Conductor detects drift and keeps the record aligned with reality; this is continuous leadership, not just periodic checking.

## Producer — owns investment and value [#producer]

Decides whether the work earns its keep: what to fund, continue, reshape, or stop. Uses evidence of value, cost, and alternatives to judge investment within the strategic direction.

Producer can decline an investment that fits strategy but costs too much. Director can change a strategy even when individual investments show positive returns. Evidence from investment reviews informs Director's judgment without making funding decisions a substitute for strategy.

## Promoter — owns adoption [#promoter]

Helps the intended audience understand and use what was built. Owns positioning, communication, documentation, enablement, and evidence of adoption. Delivery and acceptance alone do not establish that the work is being used.

## Decision boundaries [#boundaries]

| Decision | Accountable role |
| --- | --- |
| Does the direction still make sense? | Director |
| What system or capability should serve it, and how does it hold together? | Composer |
| How should work and resources be arranged? | Orchestrator |
| How do we keep execution moving within the agreed boundaries? | Conductor |
| Is the investment worth its cost? | Producer |
| Are the intended people understanding and adopting it? | Promoter |

For example, when customer needs change, Director revisits the direction, Composer revises the capability definition, Producer reassesses the investment, Orchestrator rearranges resources, Conductor leads the transition, and Promoter supports adoption.

## Scope and altitude [#altitude]

The same six roles apply at capability, system, and portfolio scope. Altitude changes what a role answers for. It does not create another method or a different role set.

A Director at portfolio scope tests the direction across systems; at system scope, the role tests that system's direction within the strategy above it. A change beyond that authority is escalated. The same principle applies to every role.

## Mapping to conventional roles [#mapping]

These are adoption aids; job titles often span several Harmonic Method roles.

| Harmonic Method role | Common holder |
| --- | --- |
| Director | Business leader or strategy lead |
| Composer | Product lead, architect, or system designer |
| Orchestrator | Engineering or delivery planner responsible for capacity and coordination |
| Conductor | Tech lead or delivery lead directing day-to-day execution |
| Producer | Business owner, sponsor, or leader accountable for investment value |
| Promoter | Product marketing, enablement lead, or internal adoption champion |

## Agents and skills [#assistants]

Agents can assist every role with research, drafting, assessment, monitoring, and delegated execution. An agent may perform role responsibilities under a named person's accountability. Decision authority and approval remain with that person unless explicitly delegated within agreed limits.

We recommend defining skills for the work an agent performs: required context, expected outputs, quality checks, permitted actions, and when to ask the accountable person. A role name alone is not an instruction or permission to act.
