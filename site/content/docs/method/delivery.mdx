<p className="cajon-page-lead">The work-breakdown the Orchestrator arranges against. None of it presumes software — a Work Item is delivered in code only when that happens to be the nature of the work.</p>

The [method primitives](/docs/method/primitives) work across tools. The commercial structures below describe one way Harmonica arranges delivery.

## Movement [#movement]

<p>The commercial container — the engagement itself, a client SOW or contract. A complete, self-contained section of a larger body of work, with its own shape and resolution. Distinct from any particular system or codebase.</p>

## Track [#track]

<p>A named grouping of commitments within a Movement — the deliverables a client is owed, grouped the way the engagement is actually sold and reported rather than the way it is built.</p>

## Work Item [#work-item]

<p>The atomic, t-shirt-sized unit of planned work within a Track. A Work Item may optionally point at a single Beat Version when the work is a software capability — a soft pointer, never a requirement.</p>

<p>That optional link is what lets this layer coordinate an engagement that is only partly software. When the work contributes to a capability, a linked Beat Version carries the plan, its Coda supplies direction, and Revisions record the changes. When it isn't — a legal brief, a campaign asset, a research deliverable — the Work Item stands on its own inside its Track, and nothing is missing.</p>

## Deliverables are not Beats [#deliverables]

<p>A <strong>Deliverable</strong> is a tangible artifact or milestone the agency hands over: a board presentation, a roadmap document, an audit report, a training session. A <strong>Beat</strong> is a durable capability the product provides its end users.</p>

<div className="cajon-callout">The recognition rule: when the work is something owed <em>to the client</em> by a date, it is a Deliverable. When it is something the <em>product</em> will let its users do, it is a Beat. Engagements typically have both, and conflating them corrupts both surfaces.</div>

## Drop [#drop]

<p>A release milestone — a named grouping of Beat Versions that ship together toward a client-visible outcome. A Drop groups work for one client account, and its readiness is computed from the state of its members rather than declared.</p>

<p>Grouping is a substantive decision, not an administrative one: what belongs in a Drop is whatever has to arrive at the same moment to be understood as intended.</p>

## Altitude, not hierarchy [#altitude-not-hierarchy]

<p>These structures describe how work is arranged commercially and released. They sit on a different axis from the primitives, which describe what the work <em>is</em>. A Movement does not contain Beats; it contains Work Items, some of which point at Beat Versions.</p>

<p>Keeping the two axes separate is what allows the same six roles to operate over a portfolio of systems, a single system, or one capability, without the vocabulary changing at each level.</p>
