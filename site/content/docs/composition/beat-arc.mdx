# The Beat Arc

<p className="cajon-page-lead">Every Capability Beat has its own lifecycle arc. Understanding that arc is how the method works in practice: not as a project-level phase a team enters and exits, but as a progression that each capability follows independently, at its own pace.</p>

## An illustrative delivery workflow

A Beat's published lifecycle is `open` or `closed`. The labels below describe an illustrative workflow within an open Beat, not additional status values. An implementation may display these labels and choose its own assessment gates. See the [published primitives](/docs/method/primitives#beat) for the entity lifecycle.

    <p>The Beat is being defined and refined. Its title and description are being sharpened, <a href="/docs/composition/key-concepts#notes">Notes</a> are being captured, and the Beat is being evaluated against the <a href="/docs/composition/quality-gates">seven quality dimensions</a>. A Beat stays in this state until it satisfies all seven.</p>

  </div>

## The arc in practice

<ol className="cajon-steps-list" aria-label="Beat arc steps">
  <li className="cajon-step-item">
    <span className="cajon-step-num" aria-hidden="true">1</span>
    <div className="cajon-step-body">
      <strong>A Beat is created</strong>
      <p>The Beat enters the system in <code>composing</code> state with a title and initial description. The title should be outcome-oriented — what the system will be able to do — not a task, feature, or deliverable name.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
  <li className="cajon-step-item">
    <span className="cajon-step-num" aria-hidden="true">2</span>
    <div className="cajon-step-body">
      <strong><a href="/docs/composition/key-concepts#notes">Notes</a> are captured</strong>
      <p>Context, constraints, decisions, and guidance that shape the capability are recorded as Notes. These accumulate throughout the arc and preserve the reasoning behind the Beat for anyone who works on it later.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
  <li className="cajon-step-item">
    <span className="cajon-step-num" aria-hidden="true">3</span>
    <div className="cajon-step-body">
      <strong>Quality evaluation runs</strong>
      <p>The Beat is evaluated against the <a href="/docs/composition/quality-gates">seven quality dimensions</a>. A Beat that fails a dimension is not discarded — it is revised until it can stand on all seven.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
  <li className="cajon-step-item">
    <span className="cajon-step-num" aria-hidden="true">4</span>
    <div className="cajon-step-body">
      <strong>The Beat transitions to <code>composed</code></strong>
      <p>Its first <a href="/docs/composition/key-concepts#beat-versions">Beat Version</a> is created, carrying description, scope, and acceptance criteria for the first planned increment of the capability.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
  <li className="cajon-step-item">
    <span className="cajon-step-num" aria-hidden="true">5</span>
    <div className="cajon-step-body">
      <strong>The first Revision ships</strong>
      <p>The Beat transitions to <code>live</code>. The capability is in production for the first time. This is the beginning of execution, not the end of the Beat's story.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
  <li className="cajon-step-item">
    <span className="cajon-step-num" aria-hidden="true">6</span>
    <div className="cajon-step-body">
      <strong>Additional Beat Versions extend the capability</strong>
      <p>Each Beat Version represents a planned increment of the capability; each Revision under it is a shippable PR. The Beat remains <code>live</code> and accumulates further Versions for as long as the capability remains relevant to the <a href="/docs/composition/key-concepts#coda">Coda</a>.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
</ol>

<div className="cajon-callout">
  The arc does not end when the first Revision ships. A <code>live</code> Beat continues to evolve — new Beat Versions are planned, new Revisions deliver them. The Beat closes only when the capability it describes is no longer needed.
</div>

## Baselining existing work

When Harmonic Composition is introduced to a project that is already in flight, some Beats will have shipped work without having gone through formal quality evaluation. Baselining brings those capabilities into the arc.

**What baselining involves:**

- Create Beats for capabilities that exist but are not yet documented in the system. Focus on outcome-oriented names — what the system does for its users, not what was built.
- Capture [Notes](/docs/composition/key-concepts#notes) for decisions already made: constraints that shaped the work, choices that were taken, context that future contributors will need. These Notes do not need to reconstruct every historical detail — they need to capture what matters going forward.
- Run quality evaluation against each Beat and refine until it passes the seven dimensions. A baselined Beat earns <code>composed</code> status the same way a new Beat does.
- Take a [Downbeat](/docs/method/cadence) to anchor the capability as it stands. Create [Beat Versions](/docs/composition/key-concepts#beat-versions) for future planned increments; do not create retrospective plans to stand in for delivered history.

**Where a baselined Beat enters the arc** depends on its current state. A capability that has already shipped enters at <code>live</code>. One that exists but has never had a Revision shipped enters at <code>composed</code> once its first Beat Version is planned. One that is only partially defined may still be in <code>composing</code>. The arc applies going forward — baselining is not a requirement to reconstruct the full history of every past decision.

---

## Multiple Beats at different arc positions

In a healthy project, Beats are at different positions in their arc simultaneously. Some are <code>live</code> and receiving new Beat Versions; others are <code>composed</code> and pending their first Revision; others are still in <code>composing</code> state being evaluated.

This is the normal state of a project. It is not a sign of disorder — it reflects the reality that capabilities develop at different paces. The relevant question at any point is not "what phase is the project in?" but "where in the arc is this particular Beat, and what does it need next?"
