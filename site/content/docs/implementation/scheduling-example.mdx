This example applies the [same primitives](/docs/method/primitives) used in the getting-started guides to staff scheduling. Vantage and the details below are fictional; they illustrate the method rather than measured results.

## The scenario

Vantage builds software for architecture firms. Customers use it for time tracking and billing, but still assign staff across active projects in spreadsheets.

The team has started a scheduling feature. Its ticket board lists a calendar view, drag-and-drop, capacity warnings, and notifications. The product lead who defined the scope has left. A new engineer asks what the system must enable, and gets different answers from different people.

## Establishing the Coda

“Make scheduling better” leaves every contributor to decide what better means. Composer defines a System Coda that serves the strategic direction:

> A project manager can assign staff across active projects, see capacity conflicts before they become scheduling errors, and understand changes that affect confirmed client commitments.

This gives the capability definitions a shared reference point. It is not a requirement to finish every part in one delivery step. Review the Coda as understanding changes, recording the decision and preserving the earlier statement.

## Capturing Notes

The team captures the context that contributors were previously expected to remember:

| Note type | Example knowledge |
| --- | --- |
| **Constraint** | A staff member cannot be double-booked without an authorized override. Changes that exceed available capacity must surface a warning before they are saved. |
| **Decision** | Show capacity in hours per week rather than a day-level available/unavailable toggle. Record the user feedback that informed the choice. |
| **Background** | Customers coordinate several active projects and currently compare separate spreadsheets to find capacity conflicts. Confirm the actual workflow with them. |
| **Guidance** | Ask for explicit confirmation when a scheduling change affects a confirmed client commitment. |

Attach each Note to what it informs. A capability-specific Note belongs with that Beat; a cross-cutting rule may belong with the System. A Note can reference the Beat Version that prompted it without attaching to the plan.

## Defining Capability Beats [#defining-beats]

Composer defines durable capabilities around the outcomes customers need:

| Capability Beat | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| **Scheduling View** | Project managers can understand staff allocation across active projects without reconstructing the picture from separate records. |
| **Conflict Prevention** | Capacity conflicts are surfaced before a scheduling change puts a confirmed commitment at risk. |
| **Change Awareness** | People responsible for a client commitment understand scheduling changes that affect it, with enough context to act. |

“Build the calendar view” is an implementation task within Scheduling View. The capability remains relevant after that task ships and can evolve through later Beat Versions.

Assess the Codas [individually and as a set](/docs/method/quality#dimensions): do their boundaries overlap, do they support one another, and do they cover the System Coda? A concrete screen design does not by itself establish a good capability boundary.

## Planning a Beat Version

Orchestrator plans a bounded first increment of Scheduling View:

- **Title:** Project managers can inspect staff allocation across active projects.
- **Scope:** Show current assignments and weekly capacity in a read-only grid. Leave reassignment, new conflict-detection behavior, notifications, and mobile editing for later plans.
- **Acceptance criteria:** All active projects and their recorded assignments are visible; each staff row shows allocated and available weekly hours; the display agrees with the authoritative records; missing capacity data is explicit; the view does not change any assignments.
- **Dependencies:** Confirm the source of assignment and capacity records, who may view them, and how current the displayed data must be.

Review the [plan quality](/docs/method/quality#gates) before execution. Future editing steps must satisfy the double-booking constraint; calling a step small does not grant an exception to it.

## Delivering Revisions

Conductor coordinates the work and its review. Contributors deliver tracked changes under the Beat Version—for example, a pull request for the read-only view and a later correction found during acceptance review. Each change points to the plan it executes.

Verify the display against the agreed records and acceptance criteria. Record consequential decisions as Notes. When the planned increment has shipped, mark its Beat Version delivered; the Scheduling View Beat stays open while its capability remains relevant.

The existing tickets can still help arrange the work. They are not automatically Revisions: the tracked changes that deliver the plan are the Revisions. Closing a ticket does not erase the capability, its Coda, or its decision history.

## The result

The team now has a shared outcome, preserved reasoning, capability boundaries, and a testable next increment. Producer can judge the next investment against evidence of usefulness and cost. That judgment is separate from whether the plan and its wording score well.
