<p className="cajon-page-lead">How the method relates to existing practice, and how its concepts apply day to day.</p>

## Questions [#questions]

<ul className="cajon-faq-list">
        <li className="cajon-faq-item">
          <div className="cajon-faq-question">Is this one method or several?</div>
          <div className="cajon-faq-answer">
            <p>One. <a href="/docs/method/overview">The Harmonic Method</a> is a single method with <a href="/docs/method/roles">five functions</a> — Composer, Orchestrator, Conductor, Producer, and Promoter — each scoped to an altitude rather than separated into sub-methods.</p>
            <p>Composition, orchestration, and conducting are ordinary English here: they name what each function does, not a separate framework to adopt. "Harmonic Methods" is the corporate name and this domain.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li className="cajon-faq-item">
          <div className="cajon-faq-question">How is this different from existing delivery approaches?</div>
          <div className="cajon-faq-answer">
            <p>Traditional and iterative approaches centre on delivery cadence and task management: time-boxed cycles, completion tracking, velocity measured through items closed.</p>
            <p>The Harmonic Method centres on different concerns — system coherence, capability evolution, and preserving intent across handoffs. The primary unit is not a task to be closed but a capability to be developed, and the primary measure of progress is alignment rather than throughput.</p>
            <p>These are complementary concerns. The method does not prescribe how a team organizes its time or meetings; it addresses what gets lost between those events.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li className="cajon-faq-item">
          <div className="cajon-faq-question">Do the five functions mean five new job titles?</div>
          <div className="cajon-faq-answer">
            <p>No. They are functions, not headcount. In a small organization one person may hold three of them.</p>
            <p>The diagnostic question is not "who has this title" but "is this function owned." Unowned functions are where decision debt accumulates — most often the <a href="/docs/method/listening">Conductor</a>, which is the function most easily assumed to be somebody's job without ever being assigned.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li className="cajon-faq-item">
          <div className="cajon-faq-question">What does altitude mean in practice?</div>
          <div className="cajon-faq-answer">
            <p>Altitude is a property of the work, not of the role. The same five functions operate at Beat, system, and portfolio altitude, and only the unit of work changes.</p>
            <p>Because the primitives are self-similar — a system is evaluated for distinctiveness and coherence exactly as a Beat is — altitude is recursion rather than a special case. A Producer at system altitude asks whether that system earns its keep; at portfolio altitude, the same question is asked across the set. <a href="/docs/method/roles#altitude">Read more →</a></p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li className="cajon-faq-item">
          <div className="cajon-faq-question">How does this help with AI agents?</div>
          <div className="cajon-faq-answer">
            <p>Agents execute well when context is explicit and the destination is clear. With a defined <a href="/docs/method/primitives#coda">Coda</a>, <a href="/docs/method/primitives#note">Notes</a> capturing assumptions and constraints, and <a href="/docs/method/primitives#beat">Beats</a> describing durable capabilities, agents have the structured context they need instead of guessing at intent.</p>
            <p>An agent will sight-read a part in the wrong key fluently and hand back confident, well-made work that does not belong. It cannot notice the clash, because clashing is a property of the whole and an agent only ever holds its part. That is the reason the quality bar travels with the work as a <a href="/docs/method/quality#gates">gate</a> rather than living in a recurring meeting.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li className="cajon-faq-item">
          <div className="cajon-faq-question">What happens to old Beats?</div>
          <div className="cajon-faq-answer">
            <p>Beats are durable by definition. A Beat stays open as long as the system depends on what it describes, and continues to evolve through Beat Versions and Revisions.</p>
            <p>When the capability is no longer relevant, the Beat is closed and its history is preserved. It becomes part of the record of what the system was and what it needed at a prior stage, which is itself context worth preserving.</p>
          </div>
        </li>
        <li className="cajon-faq-item">
          <div className="cajon-faq-question">What is the difference between a Deliverable and a Beat?</div>
          <div className="cajon-faq-answer">
            <p>A Deliverable is a tangible artifact or milestone handed over to a client — a board presentation, a roadmap, an audit report. A Beat is a durable capability the product provides its end users.</p>
            <p>When the work is something owed to the client by a date, it is a Deliverable. When it is something the product will let its users do, it is a Beat. Engagements typically have both, and conflating them corrupts both surfaces. <a href="/docs/method/delivery#deliverables">Read more →</a></p>
          </div>
        </li>
      </ul>
