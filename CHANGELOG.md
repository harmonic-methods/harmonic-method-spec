# Changelog

## v1.4.0 — 2026-08-21 (unreleased)

- **Warrant is not a score.** The root Coda instrument capped two dimensions by
  the provenance of their evidence, asserted ceilinging at 2. Six dimensions
  averaged unweighted with two held at 2 puts the ceiling on the overall at 4.0,
  so an organization without counterparty evidence could reach a threshold of 4
  only by scoring a perfect 5 everywhere else. Not a low score — an unpassable
  gate wearing the costume of one. See HM-B-012 and HM-BV-012.2.
  - **A defect this repository had already removed once.** The score floor on a
    class of Beats was deleted because six dimensions at Strong plus one at the
    sanctioned floor came to 3.86 against the same threshold. This was that
    again, worse: a ceiling not a floor, two dimensions not one, and a remedy the
    author usually cannot reach while scoring. Third time an instrument published
    here has failed this repository on first contact, and the first time by
    repeating a documented finding. See HM-N-125.
  - **A capped dimension is not underweighted, it is overweighted.** It can drag
    an unweighted mean down and cannot lift it, so it decides the outcome more
    than the dimensions that keep their range. Under the ceiling, strong scores
    elsewhere also partly compensated for having no evidence at all, because
    everything landed in one average. A band cannot be averaged away.
  - **Two bands the ceiling could not carry.** Evidence about a comparable, and
    an assumption registered with what would falsify it, who is accountable, and
    by when. Both had been stuck on an unanswerable question — what number does
    this tier cap at — which dissolves once bands stop converting to points. A
    rubric rewarding only what has been proven rewards claiming only what is
    already done; the distinction that matters is managed against unmanaged, not
    validated against unvalidated. A test that elapses unread forfeits its band,
    scoring below an honest assertion. See HM-N-126.
  - **Adoption is not value modelling.** Where a measure is well formed and its
    evidence is missing because nobody has been shown the thing, that is the
    Promoter's failure by `specs/function.yml`, and the instrument now says so.
    The two diagnoses call for opposite responses — rewrite the statement, or
    publish it and find someone — and this repository is the worked example: its
    root Coda claims adoption without an account or a call, and the repository is
    private with forking disabled. See HM-N-127.
  - **Weighting was refused.** Weights on a six-dimension rubric are
    unfalsifiable knobs, nothing here justifies a particular set, and they are how
    a rubric gets tuned until it returns the wanted answer. The mean stays
    unweighted and the gate stays explicit beside it — what band a reading must
    reach is an implementation's choice, exactly as the numeric threshold is.
  - Owed and stated: the warrant split generalizes to any instrument scoring from
    partial input, and work that resolves a load-bearing assumption still scores
    badly on the Beat rubric for being small and one-shot. Both belong to steps of
    their own.

- **What the root is held to.** `specs/note.yml` has said since the vocabulary
  landed that a Note attaches to the portfolio, system, or Beat the knowledge
  belongs to. Only the Beat case ever had somewhere to live — the third entity
  specified in full here and left unrepresentable, after the two cadence
  entities, and found the same way they were: by using the method rather than
  reading it. See HM-B-013 and HM-BV-013.1.
  - **A tightly bounded system scored 1 for having no bounds**, and the rubric
    was right. This repository's bounds are real and held on every change, and
    they lived in the contributor charter — not part of the published surface,
    read by no instrument, never seen by an adopter. A fact kept where the rubric
    cannot reach it scores as a fact that does not exist. They are now recorded at
    the root, with the ordering among them stated for the first time.
  - **The root Coda was replaced, and the prior statement is preserved** as
    `specs/coda.yml` requires. It was not wrong; it was one altitude too low. It
    described what a reader could do with a repository — its own header called it
    a system Coda — so everything beneath it laddered to distribution, and a
    capability easing adoption while leaving no organization better off had
    something to score against while one helping an organization stop work had
    nothing. The replacement is about tracing work to purpose and stopping work
    that has stopped earning its place, and it names how it can be failed. The
    exclusion that was the old statement's one strength is carried over intact.
    See HM-N-119.
  - **Published bounds count; a charter does not.** The instrument now scores the
    statement together with the context recorded at the root, and reports which
    of the two carried each dimension. An organization is bounded by what it
    published — a rule reliably honoured and never written is a habit, and
    admitting internal documents would let anyone score well on Bounded by
    pointing at something no adopter can read. See HM-N-122.
  - **One reader for both cases**, because they differ in exactly what
    `attached_to` must equal and `specs/note.yml` states every other rule once for
    all Notes. Two readers would be two places to add a rule to only one of them.
    Root Notes sit in `<root>/notes/`, a sibling of `beats/`, following the layout
    rule already in force. Both new failures were made to fail first — including
    one that was not theoretical, since the root Notes were written before the
    step they cite and the checker refused them. See HM-N-123.
  - **No spec changed.** `specs/note.yml` was already right; what was missing was
    somewhere to put an instance and something to check it, which is the cadence
    finding again and the same resolution. The spec set moves for the rubric edit
    alone, and takes a second minor step in this cycle rather than reusing the
    previous number, because the two steps merge separately and one number
    identifying two rubrics is the failure the discipline exists to prevent. See
    HM-N-124.
  - Stated rather than absorbed: the new statement names conforming
    implementations and adopter findings as its measure, and none exists yet
    beyond the worked example authored here. Under the instrument's own
    provenance ceiling that holds the two evidence dimensions at asserted. The
    score stays low until an adopter changes it, and that is the honest reading
    rather than a writing problem.

- **A root Coda that can be failed.** Four of the five accountabilities had an
  instrument and the Producer had none. The recorded reason was that its
  instrument would need Value Velocity defined closely enough to assess without
  computing it — sound, and aimed at the wrong object. A rate needs a realized
  figure and the figure arrives after the moment the judgement is needed: the
  Producer's question is whether work should continue at its current shape and
  cost, asked while the work is running. What exists at that moment is the
  statement the rate would eventually be measured against, and nothing here
  scored it. See HM-B-012 and HM-BV-012.1.
  - **The instrument scores the statement, not the rate.** Value Velocity stays
    exactly what `specs/function.yml` says it is: the measure the method names
    and the implementation computes. What is added is a rubric over the root Coda
    — the one identified by an omitted parent — asking whose benefit is claimed,
    on what evidence, inside what bounds, and what would count as the exchange
    having gone wrong. This is where the function's own failure mode lives:
    nothing gets stopped against a statement that cannot be failed. See HM-N-114.
  - **Six dimensions of its own, not the Beat's seven read higher up.** Every
    rubric here measures service to the Coda above, and a root Coda has none, so
    the heaviest dimension elsewhere has no referent and no rewording supplies
    one. Altitude is recursion for an entity; it is not recursion for a dimension
    whose referent is absent. A distinct organization altitude was weighed and
    refused — it would add an entity to carry a statement `specs/coda.yml`
    already holds. See HM-N-115.
  - **A score is capped by the provenance of its evidence.** Asserted ceilings at
    2, read out of the record at 4, generated by the counterparty at some cost to
    them at 5; a qualitative measure reaches the top tier if it names its
    observer. The instrument may not simulate the counterparty and score from
    what it imagined — that produces an assertion wearing the other party's name,
    and attaching reasoning to it makes it harder to dismiss rather than more
    true. Both rules sit inside the guarded text, so loosening either fails the
    conformance check. Each was made to fail before being believed. See HM-N-116.
  - **A drafting aid ordered from the ledgers outward**, because a destination is
    only checkable once the parties it is measured on exist. It speaks for no
    counterparty and settles nothing: an agent may draft what an organization is
    for and may not decide it, so the draft is a proposal until whoever holds the
    accountability adopts it.
  - **The spec set takes a minor step** although no spec changed apart from the
    version each repeats. `specs/conformance.yml` puts the dimensions an
    instrument scores on the comparable surface, so adding one extends what a
    declaration is made against — an addition, not a break. Earlier steps added
    skills without moving it, correctly: those produce rather than assess and
    contain nothing an implementation can contradict. See HM-N-118.
  - The Promoter still has no instrument, deliberately. Adoption rests on the
    thinnest evidence of the five and should not gain apparatus because a
    neighbour did.

- **Reading the whole system.** Every instrument here scored an entity, and the
  failure the Composer is accountable for is entirely about the set — capabilities
  overlapping, boundaries blurring, a system that works and nobody can explain. A
  portfolio of Beats each scoring four can be exactly that, and nothing detected
  it. See HM-B-011 and HM-BV-011.1.
  - **It introduces no dimension**, and an earlier reading that said it would have
    to is corrected here. The distinctiveness dimension already speaks of overlap
    with *sibling Beats*, so the set is inside its definition; reading a set is an
    aggregation of published dimensions, not a new concept needing one. Adding a
    coverage dimension would put a third definition of quality where two already
    reach, and would need anchors, a digest and parity with every implementation
    to say what two dimensions already say between them. See HM-N-112.
  - **No capacity figure, from either surface.** It is what everyone asks for and
    it cannot be honestly produced: it depends on where the boundary falls, the
    boundary is a sentence somebody can edit, and so the number rises whenever the
    Coda is widened without a single capability being added. It is the one output
    that the documented widening-to-fit failure would game. Where the space is,
    never how much.
  - **Two surfaces, kept because they disagree.** The instrument reads; the worked
    illustration does the same arithmetic on vectors the adopter supplies. Distance
    measures topical proximity rather than service, so it misses a capability that
    serves an outcome in other words; a reader glosses, so it misses ground two
    differently-worded Beats both cover. A reading that never disagreed with its
    own arithmetic would be evidence one of them was not consulted. See HM-N-113.
  - The illustration is an example and not an instrument. Vectors are input — no
    model, no network, no dependency, and reading or vendoring anything here still
    installs nothing. Its fixture is three-dimensional, hand-placed, and describes
    a system that does not exist, because inventing coordinates for a real system
    produces a real-looking finding somebody might act on. A check fails if it
    stops running or stops saying what its explanation claims.

- **A rhythm, not a reminder.** Two entities had been specified in full since the
  vocabulary landed and neither had ever existed: the checker contained no mention
  of a Bar or a Downbeat, no tree here had a place to put one, and the instrument
  that takes an anchor produced something the layout could not hold. Worse than an
  ordinary gap, because drift is the difference between the latest anchor and the
  present — an anchor never taken is the reason a Coda can move without anyone
  noticing, and the reconciliation instrument compares intent against intent up the
  chain and cannot see it. See HM-B-010 and HM-BV-010.1.
  - **Both now have a place and their rules are held.** A parent Bar contains its
    children in time; Bars sharing a parent do not overlap; an anchor names a Bar
    that exists and sits under the Beat it claims. Nesting is a parent reference
    rather than a directory, because the spec is explicit that a parent Bar is
    temporal containment at one altitude and not a change of altitude. Each rule
    was made to fail against the exact defect it guards before being believed.
  - **A period that closed with no anchor is reported, never failed.** The line is
    whether the tree is malformed or the team is behind: overlapping periods make
    "which Bar are we in" unanswerable and that is a defect, while an elapsed
    period nobody read is an accurate record of a habit not yet started. Failing it
    would refuse correct trees — the same reasoning that declined a cap on active
    steps. See HM-N-110.
  - **No cadence is added to this repository's own system**, because it runs none.
    A Bar here would claim a period work is planned within and a Downbeat would
    claim somebody took a reading; neither is true, and a fabricated anchor is
    worse than a missing one. The cost is stated rather than absorbed: this is the
    first capability the worked example demonstrates and this repository's own
    system does not, so the rules are checked against a fixture rather than
    against use. See HM-N-109.
  - Owed: a Bar's `attached_to` names the system, and the tree convention has no
    system entity — only a root Coda identifying it. The example points at that
    Coda's identifier for want of anything better, which works and is not
    obviously right.
- **Drift is a distance, and the field only asked for a flag.** The anchor entity
  contradicted itself and the contradiction had shipped. Its summary promises that
  an anchor is what makes drift *measurable* instead of remembered; its rule
  defines drift as the *difference* between the latest anchor and the present; and
  the field carrying the anchored outcome asked only for a record sufficient to
  *detect that* the outcome had changed. Detecting and measuring are different
  requirements and the field asked for the weaker one. **The spec set moves to
  1.3.0**, because an implementation storing only a digest is no longer
  conformant. See HM-BV-003.26 and HM-N-111.
  - **A digest satisfies the old wording completely** and can say nothing about
    how far or in which direction anything moved. Not hypothetical: the reference
    implementation stores a content hash and its drift call returns
    `drifted: boolean`. It is conformant and delivers exactly what was asked —
    which is how the defect stayed invisible. Nothing was wrong except the
    requirement.
  - **The form is still not mandated.** The requirement states what must be
    answerable from the record rather than what the record must contain: verbatim
    text answers it, a structured form answers it, a digest does not. Naming the
    one that fails is not naming a replacement, and none is named.
  - **Anchor the text, never a computed representation.** Where an implementation
    measures distance geometrically the temptation is to store the vector, and it
    is wrong: a vector is bound to the model that produced it, an anchor is
    immutable, so an anchored vector stops being comparable the moment the model
    changes and cannot be recomputed — the immutability that makes it evidence
    also forbids fixing it. Anchored text can be measured by any model, including
    ones that do not exist yet.
  - Owed: a recorded measure should name what produced it, the way a recorded
    score already names the spec set version it was produced under. Not written,
    because nothing yet produces such a measure and a rule designed against no
    instance is the shape HM-N-093 declines.
  - The repair could have gone the other way — weaken the summary and the rule to
    match the field. That would have kept every implementation conformant and
    reduced the anchor to a change flag anyone could get by comparing two records
    directly, making the entity redundant.

- **What nobody decided.** The composing instrument stops rewording when a
  dimension resists twice, on the reasoning that a dimension which will not lift
  is rarely a sentence problem — and then said nothing about what to do next,
  leaving the person where the score already left them: told their Beat is weak,
  not told what they have to decide. The clarifying instrument is that next step.
  It reads the dimensions that scored low and turns each into a question their
  author can actually answer. See HM-BV-009.2.
  - **The division it depends on**: revision fixes what is badly written, where
    the knowledge exists and the wording fails to carry it; clarification asks for
    what is not known, where no wording helps because nobody has decided the
    thing. A draft reworded around a gap reads better and says no more, which is
    worse than the draft that showed the gap.
  - **Questions are derived, never tabulated.** Each comes from the rubric's own
    text at the moment of asking — the dimension, its red flag, and the anchor one
    band above where the score landed. A stored mapping from dimension to question
    would be a compressed statement of what a dimension means, which is the second
    copy HM-N-012 forbids, and it would drift silently: nothing fails, the
    questions simply stop matching the rubric that produced the score they answer.
    A stock question also asks what a dimension usually lacks, where a derived one
    asks what *this* Beat lacks. See HM-N-107.
  - **Every question carries a proposed default**, because an open question and a
    proposal are not the same act. "Where should the boundary be?" is postponed;
    "I would draw it at X — is that right?" gets checked. Being wrong in the
    proposal is not a cost, it is often what produces the sharpest correction —
    the same move the composing instrument already makes when it marks an
    assumption rather than leaving a blank. The list stays short because a long
    list is a form, and a form does not get filled in. See HM-N-108.
  - **Some low scores are not questions.** A dimension that will not lift is
    sometimes a boundary in the wrong place, and asking a question implies an
    answer would fix it. Those are reported as findings naming the capabilities
    the instrument thinks are present, with the redraw left to a person.

- **An anchor that can be wrong.** The method names drift as the Conductor's
  central value and specifies the entity that makes drift computable in full, and
  nothing here produced one. HM-N-036 cleared the ground for this instrument when
  it refused a codebase-probing dimension in the plan rubric — assessing
  realized-against-planned belongs to a cadence anchor, not to a rubric scoring a
  plan — and the ground stayed clear. See HM-BV-001.4.
  - **It defines no convergence scale, and that is the point.** `downbeat.yml`
    says how convergence is computed and on what scale is an implementation's,
    which is not a gap someone forgot to fill: a percentage of planned scope means
    something for software and nothing for an organizational practice, and leaving
    it open is what lets the entity hold everywhere. Every other instrument here
    carries a scale, so one without looks unfinished beside them — and adding one
    would make a number canonical that the spec said was theirs. Worse, a reading
    that looks like a score invites comparison between systems meaning different
    things by it, manufacturing exactly the false equivalence parity exists to
    prevent. See HM-N-105.
  - **A reading of the record is not a reading of the capability.** The spec says
    a Downbeat records where a capability *honestly* stood, and an agent can see
    what the record claims rather than what is true. This repository has produced
    an instance of each direction: thirteen steps once sat marked in flight with
    their work already on the trunk, and in the same reconciliation one step read
    as further along than it was because its work merged into a branch that had
    itself already merged. So the instrument reports what it examined and what it
    took on the record's word as two lists, and says what it could not determine.
    That split is also the mitigation for having no scale — prose can be written
    loosely, an enumeration of what you actually looked at much less so. See
    HM-N-106.
  - It refuses to sign an anchor. `taken_by` is a person, or an agent under a
    named person's accountability, and an anchor nobody answers for is not
    evidence. It also never edits a prior anchor, never grades, and takes one
    capability at a time — a single reading across a portfolio averages away the
    only thing worth knowing.

- **The published command runs as published.** The front page tells an adopter to
  point the checker at their tree with an absolute path, and an absolute path did
  not work: the argument was joined onto the caller's working directory, which
  relocates an absolute path underneath it. The checker then looked somewhere that
  does not exist and reported no Coda — naming the path the adopter supplied,
  which exists and which they can see exists. Someone following the instruction
  exactly was told their tree was malformed, by the capability whose whole point
  is that they can verify a claim themselves without asking anyone. See
  HM-BV-006.8 and HM-N-104.
  - **The tool was corrected, not the instruction.** Rewording the front page to
    instruct a relative path would have taken a line, passed every check, and left
    a checker that mishandles an ordinary argument with the documentation shaped
    around the defect so nobody would find it again. Narrowing the command to the
    form that happens to work narrows the capability and calls it a fix.
  - **It was found by using the instrument on something real**, not by reading the
    code and not by a check — dogfooding a different capability against a tree in
    a scratch directory, which was the first time anything here had been pointed
    at a tree by absolute path. Every run inside this repository takes the
    relative form, and so would every example an author tries while writing the
    instruction. A defect reachable only from outside survives any amount of
    internal use.
  - The guard spawns the published command rather than calling the function, in
    all three forms — absolute, relative, and a directory holding no Coda. The
    third is there because a resolution change making every path appear to exist
    would satisfy the first two and be worse than the defect it replaced. It was
    made to fail against the original defect before being believed.

- **The root outcome claims what the repository does, and only that.** It was
  wrong in both directions and neither error showed until something was measured
  against it. It claimed too little: it promised a reader could read the model,
  name the functions and assess work, and said nothing about producing any of it
  — so once an instrument that composes and corrects entities exists, the outcome
  stops describing the repository, and every step serving that capability scores
  as indirect progress toward an outcome that does not reach it. That is the
  ninth reconciliation check, and it fired one altitude down before anyone looked
  up. See HM-BV-007.3 and HM-N-103.
  - **It also claimed too much, which is the worse half.** It promised a team
    would "get the same answer any conforming implementation would give". Scores
    here come from an agent reading a rubric, and HM-N-081 had already refused to
    record a score as a field because an LLM score is not reproducible and doing
    so would give a non-deterministic judgement the authority of a field. The
    root was granting that same judgement the authority of a guarantee. An
    argument sustained about a field does not stop applying because the claim
    moved up an altitude.
  - **It overclaimed against its own child.** The capability that actually
    delivers parity promises a score *means the same thing* wherever it is
    produced, so nobody re-learns what a 4 is. Meaning the same thing and being
    the same answer are different promises, and the parent held the stronger,
    falser one. The root now says what the child can show.
  - The trade is recorded rather than hidden: a widened outcome is a weaker one,
    and the fifth reconciliation check exists for that. The clause added is narrow
    on purpose, and an outcome that silently excludes a capability the repository
    ships teaches every future reader that the outcome is decorative.

- **The repository drafts what it grades.** Three instruments score a Beat, a
  plan, and a chain of outcomes, and none of them helped write any of it — so
  someone arriving with a paragraph had to hand-author the shape from the worked
  example before the rubrics had anything to read, putting the hardest part of
  adoption before the part this repository supports. The first producing
  instrument closes that for the Composer: read the specs, elicit what is
  missing, draft the Coda and then the Beat, and score the result with the
  published rubric read as written. See HM-B-009 and HM-BV-009.1.
  - **It revises as well as drafts**, on two loops with deliberately different
    limits. Its own is bounded at one pass, because a draft iterated against a
    rubric the same agent is applying measures the agent rather than the Beat. The
    author's is not bounded at all — bounding it would be the instrument overruling
    the person it exists to serve. Where an instruction contradicts the score, the
    person wins and the cost is named, because quietly splitting the difference
    leaves a draft satisfying neither and a reason satisfying nobody. Revision sits
    in this step rather than a later one because a draft is not a record: the
    lineage obligation that makes amendment hard binds a file somebody has relied
    on, and amending anything already recorded is refused outright.
  - **It defines no quality of its own**, which is the whole discipline. It
    restates no dimension, anchor, red flag or threshold, and points at the
    canonical instrument instead — the cheapest way to honour HM-N-012's rule
    against a hand-maintained second copy is to keep no copy at all. Its three
    standing moves are ask, mark unresolved, or stop; inventing a fact about a
    system nobody has seen is the one failure it could introduce into a record
    whose purpose is that reasoning stays recoverable.
  - **Nothing in it is normative, so the digest does not cover it** and nothing is
    left unguarded by that — there is nothing in it to guard. Widening the guard
    over drafting prose was refused: the tool's own reasoning is that hashing text
    which churns for unrelated reasons trains the reflex of bumping a digest
    without reading the change. HM-N-101 states what would reverse this — a
    producing instrument that decides something the rubric does not.
  - Production is a capability of its own rather than a step under scoring, whose
    constraint was written for assessment. That raises a question about the system
    Coda, which promises reading, naming and assessing, and does not mention
    producing. Raised in HM-N-100 and deliberately not settled by a skill.

- **The right second instance.** The decision that a dimension is never a type
  cited two cases. One was wrong about the system it described: the reference
  implementation's decision tier — `strategic`, `structural`, `implementation` —
  lives on a separate optional field, documented as "only meaningful on decision
  Notes", and those words appear nowhere as a type value. The implementation
  already does what the rule prescribes, so the Note cited a compliant system as a
  violation. Found by reading the code the Note described, not by a check.
  A better instance was in the same enum unnoticed: the `document` type carries a
  storage key, a MIME type, a processing status and an assignment status, and other
  Notes reference it to record where they came from — provenance and processing
  state, both on this spec's own list of dimensions an implementation owns. The
  evidence that settles it is the implementation's own workaround: note listings
  pass an explicit exclude filter for this type, and a value that must be filtered
  out of listings of its own type is not a member of it. The rule does not move;
  the evidence for it gets stronger. See HM-BV-003.25 and HM-N-097.
  - The conforming path for such a value is **not** a child of a published type,
    which was the earlier assumption. Narrowing `background` with an ingestion
    record still files a dimension as a kind. Where it belongs is the knowledge
    arrival capability — and this may satisfy the condition HM-N-093 set for
    designing that entity, which is raised rather than acted on.
- **A reading is not a claim.** The declaration checker had only ever been run
  against a fictional example written by the same people who wrote the rules, so
  its blind spots were theirs and it had no way to find out. A conformance reading
  of GitHub's Spec Kit now ships beside the worked one — a real workflow, built by
  someone else, pursuing the same intent-before-change thesis and diverging from
  this method in nine specific places. It is a **reading, not a declaration**, and
  the difference is load-bearing: its maintainers have claimed nothing, and
  `specs/conformance.yml` is explicit that a declaration is the implementation's
  own claim about itself. The qualification therefore lives in the
  `implementation` field rather than in a header comment, because the checker
  prints that field and prints no comments, and a caveat that vanishes when the
  output is quoted is not a caveat. See HM-BV-006.7 and HM-N-094.
  - **The check that read one file by name now reads the directory.** The second
    declaration would have shipped unchecked and nothing would have failed — a
    passing run and a rule with no reach look identical from outside. It was made
    to fail twice before being believed: once on a fault in the second file, which
    the previous check structurally could not see, and once with no declaration
    present. See HM-N-095.
  - **The form assumes the implementation is its own author**, and nothing is
    added to answer that yet. A field naming who is claiming, or a weaker second
    document, would be designed against exactly one case written by this
    repository — the weakest possible evidence, and the test HM-N-093 already set
    for a different owed shape. What would settle it is a reading produced by
    someone with no hand in this specification. See HM-N-096.
  - Three findings the comparison surfaced belong to other capabilities and are
    owed rather than folded in: this repository publishes instruments that score
    a Beat and none that help write one; the Downbeat is specified and no
    instrument takes one, though HM-N-036 cleared the ground for it; and the plan
    rubric grades acceptance criteria that no field on the Beat Version spec
    carries.
- **One step, many changes.** How many tracked changes a planned step takes was
  stated on one explanatory surface and demonstrated on none. The specs were
  always right — `revisions` is typed as a list and a Revision executes *part of*
  a step — but the agent-facing entry point omitted the relationship, and the
  worked example showed a step with a single change, which is the shape a reader
  copies. An example with no instance of the ordinary case teaches the special
  case as the rule. The example now carries a second Revision, deliberately left
  in flight while the first has shipped, so it also shows a step part-delivered —
  the state that makes it obvious why status is carried on each change rather
  than only on the step. See HM-BV-004.6 and HM-N-099.
  - **Executing is not touching**, and two instructions in the contributor
    guidance disagreed until this said so. One held that a request spanning two
    steps is two requests; the other asks that closing the previous step be
    folded into the next substantive step's scope, which produces a request
    editing two steps every time it is followed. The check was never confused —

- **Two properties a Note has to have before anything can be built on it**, one
  added and one deliberately withheld.
  - **A Note is legible to everyone who can act on the entity it informs.** An
    implementation may classify and restrict, and every serious one will; what it
    may not do is let a Note be cited as lineage by a reader who cannot open it. A
    Note nobody can read satisfies every structural rule and defeats the claim the
    method rests on — worse than an absent Note, because the citation resolves and
    the reader concludes the reasoning exists and is simply not for them, which
    stops the question being asked again. Where a restriction genuinely forbids
    that, the readable Note carries the decision and enough reasoning to act on,
    and the restricted material is evidence rather than the record. No
    classification scheme is specified: the tiers vary by organization and by
    regulation, and the bound is what protects the lineage under any of them. See
    HM-BV-003.19 and HM-N-073.
  - **Notes do not expire.** No time to live, no ephemerality flag, no automatic
    disposal — the Note's first rule is that it is preserved, and lineage that
    disposes of itself is a log. The need behind the proposal is real and is
    answered elsewhere: the reference implementation already carries a
    pre-promotion triage state, so disposable capture exists as a stage before a
    Note rather than as a property of one. Retention is explicitly not foreclosed,
    and the distinction to start from is recorded — expiry disposes of a record
    because time passed, retention because an obligation says it must be gone. The
    unaddressed case is named rather than left to be discovered: what happens to a
    decision whose cited evidence is destroyed under obligation. See HM-N-074.
- **`context` stops being one of the four Note types.** The type for knowledge
  that rises to no narrower kind is now **`background`**, and no type is called
  context — every Note is context in the general sense, and the word for the whole
  cannot also be the word for one of its parts. The previous step declared the
  hierarchy and kept `context` as a value in the same breath, which made filtering
  ambiguous; it named that as the one thing it left open, and this closes it.
  Renaming the value rather than the category is deliberate: the claim that every
  Note *is* context is what lets the type be closed and a child degrade
  gracefully. `background` was chosen over `information`, which names the category
  again from another direction, and over `general`, which describes a position in
  the hierarchy rather than what the Note holds. Thirteen Notes carried the old
  value and were retyped in the same change — a rename that leaves the data behind
  is not a rename. See HM-BV-003.18 and HM-N-072.
  - **The checker now reads the permitted types from the spec.** Its own failure
    message says a type "is not in specs/note.yml" while the list came from a
    hand-written constant beside it. A rename that has to be made in two places is
    when a restated fact goes wrong, so it is read rather than restated —
    confirmed by changing the spec's enum and watching every Note fail against it.
  - The pattern this rename fixes has now appeared three times in one cycle: a
    description corrected while the value it labels kept asserting what the
    correction removed. A retired altitude in a field description, a rubric
    dimension de-commercialised in body but not in name, and this. Two of the
    three were found by reading, not by a check.
- **Note types close, and gain children instead.** An implementation needing a
  finer kind narrows a published value through an optional `subtype` rather than
  adding a fifth type nobody else can read. This reverses the half of the previous
  decision that opened Note types, and reverses it on that decision's own
  argument: openness was justified by an added type costing a reader little, since
  the content still reads — but a child costs less on the same axis, because an
  unknown child still reports its parent, so a reader loses precision rather than
  the classification outright. Every enum now declares `children` beside
  `additions`; they are different questions with different answers on the same
  enum, and Note types are the proof — closed to siblings, open to children. The
  four types are also declared a hierarchy rather than a list: every Note is
  context in the general sense, and `context` as a value means one that rises to
  no narrower kind. `hm conformance` gains a `child_type` extension kind, and the
  worked example now narrows `guidance` rather than adding a type. What this leaves
  unresolved is recorded rather than smoothed over — `context` now carries two
  senses, and the rename is a separate step because it moves a published value.
  See HM-BV-003.24 and HM-N-071.
- **A conformance claim now has a shape, and a check.** The statement said what a
  claim covers and left the claim shapeless, so two implementations could both
  declare conformance and mean different things, and neither could be
  contradicted. A declaration requires the spec set version, who is claiming, and
  every known contradiction; extensions and vocabulary mappings are optional,
  because they do not change what a claim is worth. An empty divergence list is
  the strong claim — omitting the list is not the same as declaring it empty, and
  the check draws that distinction. `hm conformance <file>` checks the form, and
  is most pointed about the move a declaration would most plausibly be used to
  disguise: an added value on an enum declaring `additions: none` is reported as a
  contradiction filed under the wrong heading. It cannot confirm behaviour, and
  says so in its own limits. See HM-BV-006.5 and HM-N-069.

    entry per entity naming every field carried and every value permitted — a
    mirror of the specs that nothing read and that would drift from them silently.
    What a reader needs is the delta. The surface sentence promising a check over
    fields, required-ness and permitted values is corrected in the same step to
    say what is true, rather than left asserting coverage the tool does not
    provide.

- **Encoding does not bind, and the checker now says so.** No spec file defines a
  directory layout, a file format, or an identifier scheme, yet the checker
  enforces a layout on any tree it is pointed at, and the published surface listed
  that layout as binding — the third and largest instance of a checker stricter
  than its spec. Resolved by declaring the split rather than weakening the
  checker: the *relationships* the specs state are what bind — which entity a Note
  attaches to, which Beat a Beat Version belongs to, which Beat Version a Revision
  executes, which Coda a Coda ladders to — and the *encoding* does not. A method

  format, and an implementation keeping its records in a database would otherwise

- **Every enum declares whether additions are allowed.** An added value fell in
  neither category the conformance statement defined — the contradiction list
  covered a value renamed or collapsed, never one added. Note types are open, so
  an implementation defining a further kind of knowledge is extending the method;
  the four status enums are closed, because an unknown status cannot be
  interpreted — a reader cannot tell whether a fourth value means done or dropped.

  rather than inferring it, because the question is easy to miss when adding a
  field and closed-by-default is a silent answer. A second rule bounds the

    registry, which only ruled it out as a package name: a binary name needs no

    deliberately not used for the command: a scope guarantees a name forever,
    which matters for what an implementation depends on and less for what a person
    types, and it lengthens the very form a standing constraint requires be kept
    comfortable. Owning a scope does not reserve the unscoped name — separate
    namespaces, and only the unscoped one can be taken by someone else. See
    HM-N-059.
  - **A real defect caught only by installing from a packed archive**: the parser
    both the command and the checker import at runtime was declared as a
    development dependency, so the package worked here and would have crashed on
    first use once installed. Verify a package by installing it, never by reading
    its manifest. See HM-N-060.
  - **Not published.** That needs credentials and is a release action. Scoring
    this step against its Coda returns only adequate for exactly that reason, and
    the shortfall is recorded rather than absorbed — a packaged command nobody can
    install is inert. Third time a mechanism has landed a cycle ahead of what
    makes it usable, after a pinnable version with nothing to pin and an entry
    claiming release with no tag. See HM-BV-006.2.
- **The checker takes a root.** `node tools/validate.mjs <path>` now checks any
  tree, not only this repository's. With no path it self-checks exactly as before.
  The front page no longer has to admit that the checker cannot check your tree.
  - **Deciding what does not travel was the substance.** Checked anywhere:
    required fields, lifecycle values, decision rationale, reference resolution,
    and each child's agreement with its directory — what the specs require.
    Not checked elsewhere: the forbidden-vocabulary sweep, the instrument
    digests, the version agreement, the commit trailers. Those are disciplines
    this repository keeps on itself, and running the vocabulary sweep on an
    adopter's tree would fail them for writing ordinary English. A repository may
    hold itself to more than it asks of others, and the tool has to tell the two
    apart or the stricter set silently becomes the standard. See HM-BV-006.1 and
    HM-N-058.
  - The root Coda's identifier is read from the tree rather than asserted; only
    this repository knows its own.
- **The checker stops requiring what the spec does not.** `specs/beat.yml` lists
  a Beat's planned steps as optional; the checker rejected any Beat carrying
  none. The effect was not cosmetic: the method requires a Beat to pass its
  quality check *before* being decomposed, and the tree could not hold a Beat in
  the state that gate governs — scored, not yet decomposed — so a Beat and its

  divergence as an identifier the checker demanded while the spec never declared

  defect in the checker.
- **`HM-B-006`, conformance anyone can execute (new Beat, not yet decomposed).**
  Scored 4.0 against the published Beat rubric, and the weak dimension —
  Harmonious at 3 — was fixed before decomposition rather than after: the Beat
  now names the standing constraint it is held against, that reading, applying,
  and vendoring must continue to install nothing. HM-N-056 draws that boundary:
  publishing a command is permitted, requiring it is not.
- **No change to the spec set**, which stays at 1.1.0.
